Blob Anatomy

A brain made of bits that never stops ticking. This page shows what is in it, which brain mechanism each part stands for, and why each part is shaped the way an ordinary computer likes. Written 2026-11-24, before the first build.

The sheet: who a neuron listens to

One slot: the whole computation is AND and popcount

// per slot: s = the source tile's spike word, inh = its inhibitory word
e = s & ~inh                         i = s & inh
excite  = popcount(e & w0) + 4 * popcount(e & w1)
inhibit = popcount(i & w0) + 4 * popcount(i & w1)
branch  = excite - inhibit_gain * inhibit
if (excite >= branch_threshold) branch += branch_bonus   // inputs together on one branch count extra
drive  += branch

// per neuron, after its 11 slots
potential  = potential - (potential >> leak_shift) + drive
would_fire = potential >= threshold + sector_bias && cooldown == 0

One tick, forever

Learning, with no gradients and nothing stored per synapse

Each neuron already has one bit that says "I fired in the last few ticks". Every rule below is that bit, the weight masks, and a sparse random word. A change is always one weight level up or down, taken by chance; the chance is the learning rate.

// recent = sources in this slot that fired lately    conn = w0 | w1    chance = sparse random word

// 1. predict your own input (always on, needs no reward)
sensor fired, as the blob guessed or not:   strengthen  recent & conn & chance
blob guessed it would fire, it did not:     weaken      recent & conn & chance
surprise = popcount(predicted ^ actual)     // per sector

// 2. good and bad (a broadcast to the whole blob, for a few ticks)
for neurons that fired lately:   good: strengthen  recent & conn & chance
                                 bad:  weaken      recent & conn & chance

// 3. grow toward what is active near you (feature locality)
on firing, by chance:   pick one bit of  recent & ~conn   and wire it in at strength 1

// 4. prune what does not help
on firing, by chance:   weaken  conn & weak & ~recent & chance   // strong connections are exempt

Open question, stated plainly: whether prediction plus one good/bad broadcast produces purposeful play is not known. The first milestone is one blob visibly learning a one-move task.

Grid cells, mirror neurons and columns are not on the list. They are things neurons end up doing, so we look for them in the viewer; we do not build them.

Why this shape suits the machine

Spike history, 4,096 neurons8 KB of a 48 KB L1 cache
All weights, 4,096 neurons721 KB of a 1 MB L2 cache (one core)
All weights, 100,000 neurons17.6 MB of a 64 MB L3 cache
  • Same shape everywhere. Every neuron is 11 slots of 2 words, so the loop is the same instructions for every neuron. That is what SIMD wants: AVX-512 counts 8 words per instruction on the Ryzen 9950X.
  • Cost follows neuron count, not its square. 44 AND-plus-popcounts per neuron per tick at any size.
  • It is a stencil. A tile computes from itself, its 8 neighbours and at most two far words per neuron. GPUs and FPGAs are built for that. The random part is what is in the masks, never the shape of the circuit.
  • Learning is rare. Masks are only edited for neurons that just fired, and reward only acts on reward ticks.
  • The genome is small. About 50 to 200 integers describe how a blob is born. Thousands of blobs fit in memory for a later search.

Measured on the earlier all-to-all prototype, one core, plain C: 1,024 neurons at 110,000 ticks a second, 4,096 at 11,000, and 16,384 at 252 because the masks no longer fit in cache. That cliff is why wiring is local. The tiled layout on this page has not been measured yet.
